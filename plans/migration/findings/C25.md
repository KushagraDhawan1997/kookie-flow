## What a person experiences

Three different things, only one of which is slow.

Fast and severe: a consumer who passes `font={{ name: 'X', weights: {...} }}` as an object literal to `<KookieFlow>` leaks two 512×512 RGBA atlas textures (~2 MB of GPU memory) on **every render of their parent component**, because `useEffect(..., [font])` at FontContext.tsx:137 re-runs on the new object identity and nothing releases what it replaces. That is a tab crash in minutes, not a slow leak, and it is reachable from the documented public API with no warning.

Slow and unbounded: a long-lived editor that toggles light/dark retains ten `ShaderMaterial`s per flip — with their uniform objects and their several-kB GLSL source strings — in three's own strong `WebGLShaderCache.materialCache` Map, forever. Roughly 40-60 KB of JS heap per toggle that GC cannot reclaim, plus program and shader-stage refcounts that can never fall to zero, so the compiled GPU programs survive even a full unmount of the flow.

Per mount/unmount (route changes, or a host that conditionally renders the canvas): 12 geometries, 12 materials and 2 atlas textures leak per `<KookieFlow>` lifetime. `text-renderer.tsx:559` and `text-entities.tsx:484` both mount their semibold weight mesh conditionally on `hasSemiboldEntries`, so on those two the material+geometry pair leaks every time bold text appears and then disappears — during ordinary editing, not just on teardown.

Nothing is visibly wrong on screen at any point, which is why 196 unit laws and 37 behaviours are green over it.

## Proposed fix

Three parts. Nothing needs restructuring; the memos stay exactly as they are.

(1) Ten dispose effects, one line each, placed immediately after the memo they guard. Keyed on the memoised value so the dep array cannot drift from the thing it frees:
`useEffect(() => () => material.dispose(), [material]);`
at nodes.tsx (after :315), sockets.tsx (after :249 and :265, one effect each for `bgMaterial`/`fgMaterial`), entity-selection.tsx (after :160 and :283), text-edit-cursor.tsx (after :115 and :130), reroute-nodes.tsx (after :136), selection-box.tsx (after :103), grid.tsx (after :92). Two more for the unmount-only sites: text-renderer.tsx (after :103) and text-entities.tsx (after :92) — material AND geometry there, since those two components unmount during editing.

(2) Geometry teardown, one effect per file, on the `[]`-memoised geometries: nodes.tsx:111/112, sockets.tsx:152/153, entity-selection.tsx:58/217, text-edit-cursor.tsx:132, selection-box.tsx:22, reroute-nodes.tsx:62. `useEffect(() => () => { bgGeometry.dispose(); fgGeometry.dispose(); }, [bgGeometry, fgGeometry]);`

(3) edges.tsx:416 — split the dispose out of the subscription effect entirely rather than only narrowing its deps. The subscription effect keeps `[store, ...]`; a new `useEffect(() => () => { bgMaterial.dispose(); fgMaterial.dispose(); }, [bgMaterial, fgMaterial])` owns the teardown. Narrowing to `[bgMaterial, fgMaterial]` as filed would leave the two concerns in one effect and re-invite the same mistake; splitting makes the dep array unable to lie.

(4) FontContext.tsx, two changes:
  - Do NOT dispose in the loader effect's cleanup. Add a separate effect that runs *after* the new state has committed:
    ```
    const liveRef = useRef<THREE.Texture[]>([]);
    useEffect(() => {
      const next = [fontState.regular?.texture, fontState.semibold?.texture].filter(Boolean) as THREE.Texture[];
      const prev = liveRef.current;
      liveRef.current = next;
      for (const t of prev) if (!next.includes(t)) t.dispose();
    }, [fontState]);
    useEffect(() => () => { for (const t of liveRef.current) t.dispose(); }, []);
    ```
  - Stop reloading on object identity. Key the loader on a structural signature and read `font` through a ref:
    `const fontKey = isPreset(font) ? font : \`${font.name}|${font.weights.regular.atlasUrl}|${font.weights.semibold?.atlasUrl ?? ''}\`;` with the loader effect keyed `[fontKey]`. This fixes it for every consumer instead of asking every consumer to memoise. Add the missing `if (cancelled) return;` guard before `setFontState` in the custom-config branch (FontContext.tsx:120-125) so a cancelled custom load does not orphan the two textures it just built.

## What could regress

Four real hazards, in descending order of how badly they bite.

FontContext ordering — the one the audit names, and it is real. React runs the previous effect's cleanup during the commit of the render in which `[font]` changed, and `setFontState(INITIAL_FONT_STATE)` runs in the *new* effect body right after it. Between those two moments the four live weight meshes (`text-renderer.tsx:562/612`, `text-entities.tsx:479/485`) still hold the old texture in their `uAtlas` uniform, and `frameloop="always"` means a rAF can land in that window. Disposing there frees a texture four live materials name. This is why the fix disposes from a `[fontState]`-keyed effect (which cannot run before the new textures have committed) rather than from the loader's cleanup.

Never dispose the atlas texture from the weight meshes. `text-renderer.tsx:89` and `text-entities.tsx:78` receive `texture` from `LoadedFontWeight`, which is owned by FontContext and is simultaneously handed to up to four mounted meshes; `text-renderer.tsx:660 const atlasTextureCache = new Map<string, Promise<THREE.Texture>>()` additionally hands the same texture to any future `TextRendererLoader` mount. Disposing it there is a re-upload per mount today and permanently black text the moment C26's `bitmap.close()` lands next to it. Dispose only `material` and `geometry` at those two sites.

Dispose keyed on a per-frame value. `material.dispose()` is recoverable in three (the next render re-inits and re-acquires), so a dispose wired to something that changes every frame produces no visual defect at all — it produces a silent per-frame recompile/re-upload. That is exactly the failure this repo cannot see by eye, and it is why the dep array must be the memoised object itself and nothing else.

edges.tsx double-dispose. `onMaterialDispose` (WebGLRenderer.js:1114-1122) removes its own listener, so a second `dispose()` on the same material is a no-op — but `getProgram` re-adds the listener on the next render (WebGLRenderer.js:2147), so repeated store swaps under the current `[store, bgMaterial, fgMaterial]` really can decrement `usedTimes` more than once and reach `program.destroy()` while a live material still points at it. Splitting the effect removes the possibility rather than narrowing the window.

Which existing law catches a mistake: `harness/spikes/theme-flip.mjs` is the one that bites — its null control (`stable`), its node-ink check (`survived`) and above all its light→dark→light round trip (`recovered`, exact equality on canvas ink, no threshold) all fail if a geometry a live mesh is drawing gets freed. `harness/behaviors.mjs`'s 37 pointer/keyboard laws fail if text or sockets stop drawing. `harness/spikes/counts.mjs` is what catches the per-frame-thrash form: a dispose/re-upload loop shows up as draw-call and allocation churn per FRAME, which is the column that spike exists to report. Note that theme-flip.mjs will NOT catch a wrongly disposed *material* — three recovers from that — so the material half is exactly the half that needs the new law below rather than an existing one.

## What the critics said

### exists

The defect stands, but two sub-claims in the harm/fix narrative are over-stated and should not go into a commit message as written.

1. "The `else` branch (custom config, FontContext.tsx:120-125) has no `if (cancelled) return` before `setFontState`, so a cancelled custom load orphans its two textures too" — NOT REACHABLE. `loadFonts` is async but the custom-config branch contains no `await`: an async function body runs synchronously to its first await, so lines 120-124 execute inside the effect body during the same tick as `loadFonts()` at :132, before the cleanup that sets `cancelled` can possibly run. The guard is dead-code robustness, not a live orphan path. (The preset branch's existing guard at :108 is also fine — it returns before `loadFontWeight` is called at :111, so nothing is built and then dropped there either.) Adding the guard is harmless; claiming it fixes a leak is wrong. Note the real dev-mode multiplier the finding does not mention: React StrictMode double-invokes the effect, so a custom config leaks two textures per mount in development regardless of cancellation.

2. "the compiled GPU programs survive even a full unmount of the flow" and the per-mount/unmount bullet ("12 geometries, 12 materials and 2 atlas textures leak per `<KookieFlow>` lifetime") — over-stated for a FULL unmount. R3F's `unmountComponentAtNode` (events-5a94e5eb.esm.js:15816) calls `state.gl.forceContextLoss()` and `_roots.delete(canvas)`, so the GL context is destroyed (driver-side textures, buffers and programs go with it) and the renderer — and with it the whole `WebGLShaderCache` including `materialCache` — becomes unreachable and collectable. I checked `dispose(state.scene)` (:176) as a possible partial rescue and it is not one: it disposes the object and its own enumerable properties, is not recursive over children, and runs 500ms later on a scene React has already emptied. So retention is for the life of the mounted renderer, not beyond it.

   What survives from that bullet, and is the sharper form of it, is the sub-claim I did verify: `text-renderer.tsx:611` and `text-entities.tsx:484` mount their semibold weight mesh conditionally on `hasSemiboldEntries` (state flipped at text-renderer.tsx:565/:595), so a material+geometry pair leaks every time bold text appears and then disappears during ordinary editing — live renderer, live context, nothing reclaimed. That plus the theme-flip retention and the font-object-identity leak are the three live harms, and none of them depends on the two over-statements above.

3. Minor: the fix list says "sockets.tsx (after :249 and :265, one effect each for bgMaterial/fgMaterial)". Confirmed correct — the two memos end at :249 and :265 — but note sockets.tsx already has three effects, and the two geometries at :152/:153 are `[]`-memoised, so part (2) of the fix is what covers them.

### safety

Three amendments the plan owes, none fatal. (a) Part 4b's fontKey is an unstated behaviour change: keying on `name|regularAtlasUrl|semiboldAtlasUrl` means a consumer who changes weights.regular.metrics while keeping the same name and atlas gets NO reload — stale glyphMap, kerningMap and metrics, silently. FontWeightConfig carries metrics and atlasUrl independently so the public type allows it; include metrics in the signature, or compare the resolved weight configs by identity. (b) That key is built on every render and the atlas URLs are data URLs: EMBEDDED_FONT_ATLAS_URL_REGULAR (core/embedded-font.ts:3372) is a single ~260KB string literal in a 671,636-byte file, so a consumer passing a FontConfig with data-URL atlases — the exact shape the package's own preset produces — allocates ~500KB per render of FontProvider, which sits above the canvas at kookie-flow.tsx:157 and re-renders with KookieFlow at 2-4 commits per gesture. Wrap the key in a useMemo on the same fields, or key on name + atlasUrl.length. The preset path (fontKey = font) is unaffected. (c) StrictMode is untested here: apps/docs/next.config.mjs:64 sets reactStrictMode: true and React 19 runs setup→cleanup→setup on mount, so every cleanup fires once against a resource still in use. It is recoverable (a disposed material re-acquires at the `programs === undefined` branch of getProgram, three.module.js:17663; a disposed geometry re-uploads from its JS arrays) but the harness cannot see it — harness/fixture/app.tsx:569-573 deliberately runs without StrictMode. Verify in apps/docs, not only the harness. Also one correction to the plan's own reasoning that does not change its action: the edges.tsx double-dispose is balanced, not dangerous, in three 0.182 — the first dispose's properties.remove makes an immediate second a no-op, and a dispose after an intervening render is matched by that render's re-acquire, since getProgram re-adds the listener and re-increments together. Splitting the effect is still correct because it removes the surface, not because the current code demonstrably over-decrements.

### minimal

Two corrections, neither of which changes the verdict, but both of which should land before this becomes a commit.

(1) THE FIX IGNORES AN EXISTING MECHANISM FOR ITS OWN SEVERE HALF. text-renderer.tsx:659-660 already holds `const atlasTextureCache = new Map<string, Promise<THREE.Texture>>()` — module-level, keyed by URL, applying the IDENTICAL configuration (flipY=false, LinearFilter/LinearFilter, generateMipmaps=false) as FontContext.tsx:52-58's loadTexture, for the same asset class. Routing loadTexture through a URL-keyed cache makes the catastrophic case structurally impossible (a new inline FontConfig with the same atlasUrl resolves to the same texture, so there is nothing to leak) and removes the fix's own top-ranked regression hazard entirely — the commit-window ordering that forces the `[fontState]`-keyed dispose effect — because nothing is ever disposed while a live uAtlas uniform names it. It also makes the missing `if (cancelled) return` orphan disappear: a cancelled load's texture goes into the cache and is reused. The trade is that the atlas is retained for the page lifetime, bounded by the number of distinct atlas URLs — which is exactly the trade this codebase already made one file over. Keep the fontKey half of (4) regardless: without it the effect still re-runs on object identity, rebuilding glyphMap/kerningMap and re-rendering every text consumer. But fontKey + the existing cache means part (4) needs no liveRef, no [fontState] diffing effect and no dispose bookkeeping at all.

(2) THE "SLOW AND UNBOUNDED" HALF IS OVERSTATED BY ROUGHLY AN ORDER OF MAGNITUDE. "with their uniform objects and their several-kB GLSL source strings ... roughly 40-60 KB of JS heap per toggle" does not follow. The shader bodies are template literals with no substitutions, which V8 hoists as constants, so all ten materials of a kind share one string; and WebGLShaderCache._getShaderStage dedupes the ShaderStage by source code independently. What is actually retained per leaked material is the material shell, its uniforms object, a handful of THREE.Color instances and one Set entry in materialCache — order of a couple of KB per toggle, not 40-60. Likewise "the compiled GPU programs survive even a full unmount" is true but bounded: byte-identical sources mean exactly ONE program per distinct shader, so the pinned usedTimes retains about ten programs, not a growing set. "Unbounded" applies to the materialCache entries, not to the programs. The severe half (font textures) and the per-mount/per-zoom half (unmount leaks, semibold remount) are correctly characterised and are what justifies the work; the theme-toggle number should be restated before it is quoted.
